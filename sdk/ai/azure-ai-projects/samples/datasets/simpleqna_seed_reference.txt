Lakeview Science Center Visitor Reference

Overview

Lakeview Science Center is a fictional public learning center located at 100 Discovery Plaza in the city of Lakeview. The center opened in 2008 and expanded with a new astronomy wing in 2021. Its mission is to make science understandable through exhibits, demonstrations, workshops, and guided programs. The building has three public floors, a rooftop observatory, an outdoor learning garden, and a separate education studio.

Operating hours

The center is open Tuesday through Sunday. Standard hours are 9:30 AM to 5:00 PM. On Fridays, the center remains open until 8:00 PM. It is closed every Monday for exhibit maintenance and staff training. The center is also closed on January 1, July 4, and December 25. The final general-admission entry is allowed 45 minutes before closing. The rooftop observatory closes 30 minutes before the rest of the building.

Admission and reservations

General admission costs 18 dollars for adults, 13 dollars for visitors ages 3 through 17, and 15 dollars for visitors age 65 or older. Children under age 3 enter free. A family pass costs 52 dollars and covers up to two adults and three children in one household. General admission includes all permanent exhibit halls and scheduled floor demonstrations. Planetarium shows require a separate 6-dollar ticket because seating is limited.

Visitors can reserve tickets online up to 60 days in advance. Same-day tickets are available at the welcome desk when capacity permits. Online reservations can be moved to another date once without a fee if the change is requested before the original visit begins. School groups and groups of 15 or more must reserve through the group-services office rather than the public ticket page.

Permanent exhibits

The first floor contains the Motion Lab, River Systems Gallery, and Inventors Workshop. The Motion Lab demonstrates force, friction, balance, and mechanical advantage through hands-on stations. Its largest exhibit is a 6-meter pendulum that traces changing patterns in a shallow sand table. The River Systems Gallery uses a 12-meter water table to show erosion, sediment transport, floodplains, and delta formation. The Inventors Workshop provides reusable building materials for designing bridges, towers, and simple machines.

The second floor contains the Energy Hall, Living Systems Gallery, and Computing Studio. Energy Hall compares solar, wind, hydroelectric, geothermal, and battery storage systems. A wall display records the output of 48 solar panels installed on the center's south roof. Living Systems Gallery explains habitats, pollination, food webs, and seasonal adaptation using models and recorded field observations. The Computing Studio introduces algorithms, pattern recognition, sensors, and robotics through guided challenges.

The third floor contains the Earth and Space Gallery and the entrance to the planetarium. Earth and Space Gallery includes a 4.5-billion-year timeline of Earth history, a collection of 27 meteorite specimens, and an interactive model of plate movement. The planetarium has 120 seats and uses a digital projection dome that is 14 meters in diameter. Planetarium programs last between 25 and 40 minutes.

Featured demonstrations

The center schedules three recurring demonstrations on most open days. The Electricity in Motion demonstration begins at 11:00 AM in Energy Hall and lasts about 20 minutes. The Chemistry of Color demonstration begins at 1:30 PM in the first-floor demonstration area and lasts about 25 minutes. The Night Sky Preview begins at 3:30 PM inside the planetarium and lasts about 15 minutes; it does not require a planetarium ticket because it uses only the first rows of seats.

Demonstration schedules can change during school-group events. The current day's schedule is posted at the welcome desk and on digital signs near each elevator. Visitors should arrive five minutes early because demonstration areas stop admitting participants when they reach their posted capacity.

Rooftop observatory

The rooftop observatory contains two fixed telescopes and six portable telescopes. Daytime solar viewing is offered only when weather and visibility permit. Staff use approved solar filters during every solar-viewing session. Evening observing events are normally held on the first Friday of each month from 7:00 PM to 9:00 PM. An evening event is cancelled when cloud cover or high wind makes observation impractical. Cancellation notices are posted by 4:00 PM on the event date.

Accessibility

All public floors are connected by two elevators. Step-free entry is available through the east entrance beside Discovery Plaza. Wheelchairs are available at the welcome desk on a first-come, first-served basis at no charge. Each planetarium program reserves six wheelchair spaces. Assistive-listening receivers can be borrowed for planetarium shows by presenting a photo identification card, which is returned when the receiver is returned.

Large-print visitor maps are available in English and Spanish. A low-sensory visit period is offered from 9:30 AM to 11:00 AM on the second Sunday of each month. During that period, exhibit audio is reduced, flashing effects are disabled where possible, and attendance is capped below normal capacity. Service animals are permitted throughout public areas, but pets and emotional-support animals are not admitted.

Food and visitor services

The Discovery Cafe is located on the first floor and is open from 10:00 AM to 4:00 PM. On Fridays it remains open until 7:00 PM. The cafe sells sandwiches, salads, fruit, baked goods, and drinks. Visitors may bring their own food, but meals must be eaten in the cafe seating area or the outdoor garden. Food and uncovered drinks are not allowed in exhibit halls, the planetarium, or the observatory.

Lockers are located near the west entrance. A locker costs 2 dollars for the day and can be reopened using a printed access code. Items larger than 55 centimeters by 40 centimeters by 25 centimeters do not fit in the lockers and cannot be stored at the welcome desk. Free water-bottle filling stations are available on all three public floors.

Transportation and parking

Bus routes 4 and 16 stop at Discovery Plaza every 20 minutes during standard operating hours. Lakeview Central Station is 0.8 kilometers from the center and is connected by a marked pedestrian route. The visitor parking garage is entered from Harbor Street. Parking costs 3 dollars per hour, with a daily maximum of 15 dollars. Members receive four hours of free parking after validating their parking ticket at the welcome desk.

The garage has 12 accessible parking spaces on level one and 8 electric-vehicle charging spaces on level two. Charging fees are separate from parking fees. A covered bicycle rack with space for 36 bicycles is located beside the east entrance.

School and group programs

School visits are offered Tuesday through Friday. Groups may choose a self-guided visit, a 45-minute laboratory workshop, or a 60-minute exhibit program led by an educator. The minimum school-group size is 10 students, and the maximum size for a single laboratory workshop is 32 students. One adult chaperone is admitted free for every 10 students. Additional adults pay the standard group-admission rate.

Group reservations require a 20-percent deposit. The remaining balance is due 14 days before the visit. A group may change its attendance count until five business days before arrival, subject to program capacity. Lunch space for school groups must be reserved in advance because the education studio seats only 80 people.

Membership

An individual annual membership costs 70 dollars and admits one named adult. A household membership costs 115 dollars and admits two named adults plus all children under age 18 in the household. Membership includes unlimited general admission, a 10-percent cafe discount, a 10-percent shop discount, and advance registration for selected workshops. Planetarium tickets and special ticketed events are not included.

Memberships begin on the purchase date and expire at the end of the same calendar day one year later. Membership cards are digital by default, although a printed card can be requested at the welcome desk. Memberships cannot be transferred to another household.

Visitor guidelines

Photography for personal use is allowed in permanent galleries unless a sign states otherwise. Flash photography, tripods, and commercial photography require prior approval. Visitors may sketch with pencils, but paints, markers, and charcoal are not permitted in galleries. Children under age 12 must remain with an adult. Lost items are held for 30 days and can be claimed through the welcome desk by providing a description of the item and the approximate date it was lost.

The center recommends allowing at least three hours for a general visit and four hours when including a planetarium show. The busiest period is usually Saturday from 11:00 AM to 2:00 PM. Tuesday and Thursday afternoons are generally quieter, except during scheduled school holidays.
